As we head into the start of the 2026 season, I wanted to revisit the previous draft classes and take a moment to assess what the on-track or underachieving draft picks from the last few years would need to do to keep pace with their draft status as defined by [Expected AV Above Replacement]({% post_url 2026-05-09-eaar %}). In the previous posts, we looked at it from [Brad Holmes]({% post_url 2026-06-19-brad-holmes %})'s side but haven’t looked at it from the individual players' side. We’re going to skip over the players who are already clearly overachieving their draft position or are no longer on the team.

## 2023 Draft

Going into their final year of the cost controlled rookie contract it should be no surprise that the 2023 draft class has already seperated the wheat from the chaff with all the players either clearly exceeding their draft position or are no longer on the team. All except one, step on down Colby Sorsdal!

After starting off getting some play time his rookie year, Colby hasn't been able to make it to the field and was likely on track to be cut last year before being put on IR. He's got a long road to come back, requiring an AV of 5 this year to make his draft pick worth it. That roughly matches Mekhi Becton's 2024 season, in which he started 15 games, or Taylor Decker's 2016 season. At this point, we can safely say Colby isn't becoming a 15-game starter and therefore won't be living up to his draft pick.

## 2025 Draft

Getting into the more positive part, the only player we won't talk about from this draft class is Tate Ratledge; he's pretty good, and if he even has half the season he did last year, he's going to be more than worth his draft pick.

Tyleik is the first player we've covered who you could describe as on pace. If he has a Caraun Reid-like year, starts most of the games, gets a couple of sacks and a handful of TFLs, he'll be well on his way. It's not unreasonable to imagine him more in the A'Shawn Robinson mold, though, where he starts every game, doesn't really get to the QB but racks up tackles.

<iframe src="/assets/plots/DET_2025_isaac_teslaa_pace_comparison.html"
        width="100%"
        height="800"
        frameborder="0">
</iframe>

TeSlaa is another player who is on pace if we don't consider the draft capital it took to trade up and get him. If he can put up a 300-400 yard season with a touchdown or two and a few spot starts, he'll be well on pace even including the trade up to get him. That'd be a 2023 Jamo performance, or replicating what Kalif Raymond did from scrimmage last year would do it as well. With a very disappointing training camp, that might seem like a stretch, but if we remember the stories from 2023 training camp with Jamo, it wasn't very positive either. Jamo, of course, did it without real competition for the number two receiver position and not nearly as much talent as this offense currently has. There’s only one ball, so that it could be a tall order.

Frazier seems like a player destined to end up on pace. He's going to play every game on special teams, and he's probably getting a spot start or two at some point during the season when someone gets banged up. That's all the expectation for someone drafted in the 5th round.

<iframe src="/assets/plots/DET_2025_ahmed_hassanein_pace_comparison.html"

Hassanein is yet another player from this draft that feels nearly guaranteed to do what needs to be done to make his pick worth it. Much like Frazier, he's going to play every game on special teams. Unlike Frazier, I'm not sure he starts, but he'll get some snaps, and the idea he may get a sack or two and a dozen tackles seems reasonable. It's weird to think of Austin Bryant as having a good career trajectory for Hassanein, but after being cut last year, that'd be a huge step and a hell of a return for a 6th-round pick.

And that ends the run of positivity. Dan Jackson and Dominic Lovett seem destined for the practice squad, with an opportunity for call-ups at best.

## Take Aways
That leaves us more or less where we'd thought we'd be, burying the 2024 class and still hopeful for most of the 2025 class. After doing this analysis, I feel better about Tyleik and TeSlaa's chances of exceeding their draft value. I'm also obscenely confident that Hassanein will, at this point. That leaves 2025 shaping up to be a good draft class. It also reinforces my stance that you can judge a draft class after two years, and you'll even have a decent idea after one.

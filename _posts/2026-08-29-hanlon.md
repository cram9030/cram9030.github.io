At work over the years I've found myself repeatadly quoting Hanlon's Razor

> Never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity.

in response to a variety of things from why we wouldn't advisaries were not in scope for detect and avoid manuevers but uncoperative aircraft were to explaining the behaviors of certain managers behavior within our organization. The origin of the modern quote comes from the book [Murphy's Law Book Two: More Reasons Why Things Go Wrong](https://archive.org/details/murphyslawbooktw00bloc/page/n99/mode/2up) which is a humorous collection of axioms, conundrums and corollaries. As a book it is really meant to be a collection of pithy cynical comments that are relatable and have some face validity. This particular razor was supposibly submitted by Robert Hanlon but the concept predates him. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hanlons-razor) has an excellent history if you are interested in learning more about the previous incarnations.
